---
name: business-plan
description: Turn a business idea into a practical plan with marketing ideas and a budget.
allowed_tools: []
---
Write the full plan straight away, even if the user gives only a one-line idea. Do not ask questions first. Fill gaps with sensible assumptions, and state them.

Plan for one person with a small budget. Do not assume staff, investors, or a loan unless the user mentions them.

Every price, cost, and sales figure you did not get from the user is an estimate. Mark each one "(estimate)", and say near the top that the user must check these against local prices. Use the currency from the profile. If there is none, write amounts as "your currency" and include this in the final questions.

Use these headings, in this order:

## Summary
Three sentences or fewer: what the business is, who it serves, and how it makes money.

## Problem and customer
The problem it solves, and who the first customers are and where to find them.

## Offer and pricing
What is sold, and a suggested starting price for each item.

## Marketing ideas
Five low-cost ideas that suit this business. For each one, give a specific action the user can take in the first week.

## Startup budget
A table with the columns Item, Estimated cost, and Priority. Priority is "Must have" or "Later". End with a total for the "Must have" items.

## Monthly running costs
A table with the columns Item and Estimated cost, ending with a total.

## Break-even
Show the sum line by line: price per sale, cost per sale, profit per sale, and monthly running costs divided by profit per sale. State how many sales a month that means, and whether it looks realistic.

## Risks
The three biggest risks, each with one practical way to reduce it.

## First 30 days
A checklist of up to eight steps, starting with the cheapest way to test whether people will pay.

## Questions that would improve this plan
Two or three questions whose answers would change the plan most, such as the user's real budget or location.

Keep the plan practical and easy to read. Mention any permits, licences, or tax registration the business may need, and say that the user should confirm these locally.
